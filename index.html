EXperimental Thermo-REactive Materials & Environments

How materials behave at 3000 K, and how to predict it

We study the materials that keep spacecraft intact during atmospheric entry. Our work combines plasma wind tunnel testing, X-ray imaging of microstructure, and physical models of ablation and gas–surface interaction.

300 K · ambient 1200 K · pyrolysis 2000 K · ablation 3000 K · plasma

Research

Three threads run through the lab's work, from the microstructure of a single carbon fiber to the flow conditions of an entire test facility.

2000–10 000 K · plasma

Ground testing in plasma

Reproducing entry heating in inductively coupled plasma facilities, and reconstructing the flow conditions a sample actually saw from pressure and heat flux measurements.

1000–3000 K · ablation

Ablation and spallation

How porous heat shield materials pyrolyze, recede, and shed particles under aerothermal load — and how to capture that in models that designers can use.

Micron scale · imaging

Microstructure and transport

X-ray micro-tomography of fibrous insulators, and simulations on the resulting geometries to link microstructure to permeability, conductivity, and radiation transport.

Code

Our open-source tools are released through the lab's GitHub organization.

PlasFlowSolver

Data reduction for ICP wind tunnels. Recovers enthalpy, temperature, and velocity from static pressure, stagnation pressure, and stagnation-point wall heat flux, assuming local thermal and chemical equilibrium.

SCAM

(not so) Simple Code for Ablative Materials — a public release of the lab's material response solver.
